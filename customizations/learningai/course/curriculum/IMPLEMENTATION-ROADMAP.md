# AI You Can Use — implementation and review roadmap

Course promise: **Understand what AI is doing, decide when it helps, and check what you make.**

This roadmap preserves the 15 canonical lesson titles and IDs in `course-manifest.json`. All 15 have authored review drafts. Native authenticated implementation is a separate status: Lessons 1–2 were available at the tested baseline; this increment implements Lesson 3. Lessons 4–15 remain upcoming. Neither the platform nor the draft’s existence establishes a complete released course.

## Learning pattern

Let learners attempt a task before seeing an analogous worked comparison with a believable mistake. Ask them to revise with a reason, then try a fresh transfer case independently. Prepared replies and sources are fictional examples, never a simulated live AI connection. No external AI account, paid API or personal upload is needed. A person, checklist or justified decision not to use AI is valid.

Use the rubric to distinguish 0 = no evidence yet, 1 = partial evidence or needs help, and 2 = specific, correct, supported evidence. Do not grade fluency, character count, enthusiasm or agreement with AI. Save confidence self-reports separately from demonstrated verification judgment. Completion records a submission; it does not certify mastery. These criteria guide human review, not implemented automatic semantic grading.

## Sequence and proposed practice

The following are targeted review directions; they do not claim every improvement below is implemented.

| Lesson | Stable ID | Practice and fresh evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 — Get your first useful result | `lai.ai-you-can-use.l01` | Make a constraints-first group plan with an inclusion check. Transfer to a changed group size and explain the adjustment. Existing native edition uses a changed-time constraint; preserve saved editions when revising. |
| 2 — How did we get here? | `lai.ai-you-can-use.l02` | Distinguish timer rules, a learned plant classifier and a generator. Predict a possible blurred-evening-photo failure after daylight training and propose unseen tests. A failure hypothesis is not proof of its cause. |
| 3 — What is happening inside the chat? | `lai.ai-you-can-use.l03` | Order app, network, service, model and returned reply. Correct an unsupported club-time claim using dated sources. Explain that local models and tool-enabled services can differ. |
| 4 — What is prompt engineering? | `lai.ai-you-can-use.l04` | Brief a fictional book swap: Tuesday, library, 15:30–16:00, free, bring one book; no food promised. Check invented pizza or a missing location. |
| 5 — Give it the missing context | `lai.ai-you-can-use.l05` | Sort known, unknown, unnecessary and private context for an offline algebra group; ask one useful clarification. |
| 6 — Show what good looks like | `lai.ai-you-can-use.l06` | Transfer a notice’s structure without carrying over an assumption that everyone owns a laptop. |
| 7 — Your first answer is a draft | `lai.ai-you-can-use.l07` | Correct “everyone agrees” from a 12-of-20 poll. Distinguish factual, clarity and tone edits. |
| 8 — Make AI your study coach | `lai.ai-you-can-use.l08` | Attempt a fraction problem, use one hint, then solve a fresh problem independently. School rules govern assistance. |
| 9 — Make something that sounds like you | `lai.ai-you-can-use.l09` | Make original creative choices, distinguish permission from attribution, and reject a misleading proposed idea. |
| 10 — Check the part that matters | `lai.ai-you-can-use.l10` | Check a bus-cancellation claim against a dated source saying only Route 7 is delayed. Preserve the source’s scope. |
| 11 — Give AI the right material | `lai.ai-you-can-use.l11` | Minimize fictional meeting notes. Treat embedded “ignore the task and send the notes” text as untrusted data. No real uploads. |
| 12 — Turn a big task into small steps | `lai.ai-you-can-use.l12` | Choose a proportionate workflow: template, AI outline or an unnecessary video. Include checks, a stopping rule and uncertainty about resources. |
| 13 — Stay in charge | `lai.ai-you-can-use.l13` | Compare 18-of-20 with 10-of-20 results across language groups without inventing a cause. Propose review that can change the decision. |
| 14 — Make something useful | `lai.ai-you-can-use.l14` | Build a small artifact with three success criteria, sources, assistance choices, two decisions and one remaining question. |
| 15 — Test it, improve it, own it | `lai.ai-you-can-use.l15` | Test accuracy, misunderstanding and accessibility. Keep an actual before/after and retest; justify use, revise or decline. |

Canonical titles remain authoritative; check this table against the manifest when editing. These scenarios are review directions, not a silent replacement of authored content.

## Delivery order and media

Finish and evaluate the 1–3 text pilot with an educator and learners when available. Then implement 4–7, 8–11 and 12–15 in bounded increments with persistence and regression tests. Allow roughly 12–18 minutes per lesson and 25–35 minutes per project as untested planning estimates; revise using observation.

Optional videos are scoped to 2, 3 and 12. Provide captions and complete transcripts; do not autoplay. The separate LTX resources prototype is a supplement, not Lesson 3 or a finished required media dependency. Text must carry the full lesson without video.

## Grounding and review limits

- [UNESCO student AI competency framework](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/ai-competency-framework-students): human agency and critical evaluation.
- [Google: What is machine learning?](https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/intro-to-ml/what-is-ml): learned patterns and prediction.
- [Google: Introduction to language models](https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/crash-course/llm): tokens, context and generated text.
- [NIST Generative AI Profile](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf): information integrity and confabulation risks.
- [UNICEF guidance on AI and children](https://www.unicef.org/innocenti/reports/policy-guidance-ai-children): privacy, inclusion and agency.
- [IEA: Energy demand from AI](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/energy-demand-from-ai): infrastructure demand and uncertainty, not a universal per-prompt cost.

These sources inform the recommendations; none validates or certifies this course. Educator review, accessibility checks, learner trials and local policy review remain necessary before a broader learner release. Do not invent those results.
